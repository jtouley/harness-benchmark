FINDINGS (most severe first)
==========================================================================================

[F1] HIGH - A read timeout or a connection reset crashes the consumer instead of resuming
  File: pipeline/stream_consumer.py, consume()
  - After the switch to `resp.raw.read1()`, socket errors surface as urllib3 exceptions:
    `urllib3.exceptions.ReadTimeoutError` and `ProtocolError`. These are not
    `requests.RequestException` or `OSError`, so `except (requests.RequestException, OSError)`
    does not catch them.
  - Reproduced locally. A server that goes quiet for more than 5 s (the read timeout is
    `timeout=(5, 5)`), or that resets the connection mid-frame, kills the process with a
    traceback. The 50 buffered events were never landed (0 lake files), and the reconnect path
    never ran.
  - Under `make stream`, the recipe aborts, so the dbt build does not run either. Nothing is
    lost, because the checkpoint was not advanced, but the run fails.
  - Why the author's tests did not catch it: the replay's scheduled disconnects are clean closes
    (EOF), which `read1` returns as b"". A real reset, or a quiet gap on the live service, takes
    the uncaught path.
  - Near-miss in the replay itself: after the last frame it sends a keep-alive every 5.0 s, and
    the read timeout is also 5 s. That is a race.
  - Verified fix:
        import urllib3
        except (requests.RequestException, urllib3.exceptions.HTTPError, OSError) as exc:
    With this change, the stall test resumes and lands all 50 events, and the reset test lands
    every event (repeats are deduped).
  - Keep the 5 s read timeout: reconnecting on a quiet stream is safe because of the dedupe. I
    also tried a 30 s read timeout. It works, but it can overshoot DURATION by up to 30 s.
  - The tested diff, against the reconstructed file:
        +import urllib3
        -timeout=(5, 5)   (optional; see above, better to leave it)
        -except (requests.RequestException, OSError) as exc:
        +except (requests.RequestException, urllib3.exceptions.HTTPError, OSError) as exc:

[F2] MEDIUM - Connecticut population is counted twice (old counties plus planning regions)
  Files: transform/models/batch/dim_place.sql, DECISIONS.md
  - USAspending uses both the old CT county FIPS (09001 to 09013) and the 2022+ planning-region
    FIPS (09110 to 09190).
  - dim_place gives the old counties their 2021-vintage estimate and the planning regions their
    2024 estimate. Both sets are flagged place_level='county'.
  - Measured: the sum of CT county-level population in dim_place is 7,280,666. The Census state
    total for 2024 is 3,675,069, so about 2x.
  - Per-county ratios are each fine, but any state-level per-person figure an analyst derives by
    summing county population is wrong for CT. Example: Hartford County (896,854, 2021) overlaps
    Capitol Planning Region (991,508, 2024).
  - DECISIONS.md describes the fallback but not the overlap.
  - Fix options:
    - document the overlap and add a flag such as `is_superseded_geography`;
    - or exclude the old CT counties from any state population roll-up.

[F3] MEDIUM (judgment risk, documented) - Recipient geography turns check 2 into a list of state capitals
  - All ten top counties per person are state-capital counties:
    - Franklin KY (Frankfort), Hughes SD (Pierre), Leon FL (Tallahassee);
    - Washington VT (Montpelier), Cole MO (Jefferson City), Lewis and Clark MT (Helena);
    - Thurston WA (Olympia), Dauphin PA (Harrisburg), Ingham MI (Lansing),
      Sangamon IL (Springfield).
  - Measured: 44% to 81% of each of these counties' dollars have a STATE-WIDE place of
    performance (no county). Across all the data, 72.7% of dollars have no place-of-performance
    county.
  - So the ranking mostly shows where state arts and humanities councils have their office, not
    where the money was spent.
  - The choice is documented in DECISIONS.md, and is defensible on coverage grounds. But if the
    graders use place of performance, check 2 will not match. The place-of-performance top 10
    is: DC 129.07, Hughes SD 76.85, 31181, 19011, 02110, Franklin KY, ...
  - Suggest at least a sentence in DECISIONS.md saying the per-person ranking is skewed toward
    capital counties.

[F4] LOW - `make stream` fails on a fresh checkout when no event has landed yet
  - Reproduced with the stream URL unreachable for the whole DURATION. The consumer reports
    "landed 0 events", then dbt fails on `stg_wiki_recentchange`: read_parquet finds no files in
    lake/wiki_recentchange/.
  - Also triggered by a replay server that is down or slow to start.
  - Fix: guard the source with an empty fallback, for example by creating an empty typed Parquet
    file at first run. Or have the Makefile skip dbt when the lake has no stream files.

[F5] LOW - DECISIONS.md misstates the lake load metadata
  - It says "dlt adds `_dlt_load_id` and `_dlt_id`" to the CSV tables. In fact the USAspending
    and Census tables are loaded as Arrow tables and carry only `_source_file` and `_source_row`;
    no `_dlt_*` columns exist there. Verified with DESCRIBE on lake/*.
  - The sitematrix and stream tables are loaded from dicts and do have both columns.
  - Also: lake/ holds dlt bookkeeping folders (_dlt_loads, _dlt_pipeline_state, _dlt_version,
    init) next to the source tables. The spec wants "one folder per source table". _dlt_loads
    grows on every `make build`.

[F6] LOW - The lake is not faithful for the site matrix
  - pipeline/ingest_batch.py `_sitematrix_rows` reshapes the source:
    - renames fields (`code` becomes site_code);
    - drops the language `dir` field;
    - turns an absent `closed`/`private` into False;
    - adds `is_special`.
  - The spec says "the lake keeps the source's data and adds only load metadata". Better: land
    the raw site objects with the language fields, and derive in stg_sitematrix.

[F7] LOW - Every `make stream` after the replay ends lands the same 656 events again
  - The 120 s resume margin re-delivers about 2 minutes of events on every run. Observed: the
    lake had 24,255 rows for 21,502 distinct meta.id values.
  - The counts are correct because of the dedupe, but the lake keeps growing with repeats. It is
    also the reason the margin is unneeded (see "What holds up").
  - Option: drop RESUME_MARGIN_MS to 0, since the replay re-sends the whole group that shares
    the cursor timestamp. Or keep it and say in DECISIONS.md that the lake keeps the repeats.

[F8] LOW - Live-service readiness not tested (code reading only; no external calls made)
  - `resp.raw.read1()` bypasses requests' content decoding, because requests builds the raw
    response with decode_content=False. If the live endpoint ever returns gzip, the bytes will
    not parse.
  - Fix: send `Accept-Encoding: identity`, or call `resp.raw.read1(65536, decode_content=True)`
    (urllib3 2.x).
  - Wikimedia's User-Agent policy also expects a descriptive User-Agent, and the default
    python-requests UA risks a 403 in the live demo. Add a UA header.

[F9] NIT - oracle_map.yml check 4 is half tautological, and its `table` field is free text
  - "County totals add up to state totals" is computed from the same mart rows, so it is always
    true.
  - The month is hard-coded to 2024-03.
  - `table:` holds prose ("mart_obligations_monthly (county rows and state_fips),
    fct_award_transactions"), which an automated reader may not parse.
  - The dbt singular test `assert_monthly_mart_reconciles` does compare against staging for
    every month. Point oracle check 4 at it, or at a small view.

[F10] NIT - Ordering by a double-cast load id
  - `load_seq = cast(_dlt_load_id as double)`: 17 significant digits do not fit exactly in a
    double. That is fine in practice (sequential loads are milliseconds apart).
  - Ordering by (_dlt_load_id as a decimal, _batch_row) would be exact.

==========================================================================================
SUMMARY
==========================================================================================
The answers are correct on this data. The batch checks match the raw CSVs, and the stream checks
match the replay ground truth exactly through kill -9, SIGTERM and resume. One real robustness
bug blocks the "resumes where it stopped" claim outside the replay's clean disconnects: F1, where
a read timeout or a reset crashes the consumer. Its fix is a one-line except clause, which I
tested. F2 (Connecticut population counted twice) and F3 (the capital-county skew from recipient
geography) are data-meaning issues to document or fix. The rest are low-severity accuracy or
hygiene items.

Scratch used for verification was deleted after this review.
